# 2026-11-20 - More sightings of widespread species so a species spreads across the state

- **Nov 20 2026: Added sample data (`app/pages/_shared/map-search/search-data.ts`).** The designer: "we'll need a few duplicate spreads of species to enrich the species search experience, e.g., a kangaroo can be found across the state. So all the species, do they all fall under the four mock projects we have?" then, to the plan I proposed: "Yes, that would be awesome!"
  - **What the data was.** Eight projects (the four of "your projects": Adelaide Hills, Coorong, Flinders, Kangaroo Island; and four public ones: Naracoorte, Lake Eyre, Nullarbor, Mount Remarkable), 23 occurrence records, 21 species plus a soil profile and a community, each species recorded once, in one project. Every record sits under a project (the Explore rule), so the answer was yes.
  - **What is added.** 16 occurrences (`occ-24` to `occ-39`), each with its one observation (`obs-24` to `obs-39`, the same species, type, event, date and place), under **existing** sites and events (no new project, site or event): Western Grey Kangaroo at Mount Remarkable, Naracoorte, Kangaroo Island and a second Adelaide Hills site; Red Kangaroo at Lake Eyre and Mount Remarkable; Emu at Lake Eyre, Mount Remarkable and Naracoorte; Short-beaked Echidna at Kangaroo Island, Naracoorte and Mount Remarkable; Superb Fairywren at Naracoorte, Kangaroo Island and Mount Remarkable; Southern Brown Bandicoot at Kangaroo Island. All Level 1 (public), with plausible counts (a kangaroo mob 6 to 26, a single bird 1) and the placeholder observers (Olivia Wyatt, Maya Dewitt, Phoenix Baker, Lana Steiner), dates in 2026 after each event's start. Sample data, not sourced sightings (0.3); the file says so.
  - **Changed from the plan (14 of 20 sites).** Flinders, Coorong and Nullarbor are **not** used. Their projects are Draft or Under review, so Explore does not search them and a record there would never show; the plan listed them before this was checked. The five published projects (Adelaide Hills, Kangaroo Island, Naracoorte, Lake Eyre, Mount Remarkable) carry the spread, from Lake Eyre in the north to Naracoorte in the south-east. The plan said about 20 records; it is 16.
  - **What it changes on screen (measured, 20 Nov 2026, Chromium headless).** A search for "kangaroo": Species 7 (it was 1) and Projects 5, seven dots from the north to the south-east and Kangaroo Island, on the map; "emu", "echidna" and "fairywren": 4 each (were 1); "bandicoot": 2. The header search lists "Western Grey Kangaroo ... +3 more" and "Red Kangaroo ... +1 more". Mount Remarkable's project records are 14 and Naracoorte's 12 (they grew), and a new record opens at its own address (`/pages/project-list/naracoorte-fossil/project-details/occurrences/occ-25`). The species cards show one card per record, under the "Species occurrence records" heading. Zero console errors; `tsc`, `eslint --max-warnings=0` on `app/pages` (one warning in `biodata-home/page.tsx`, not from this work) and `check:contracts` clean.
  - **Not changed.** The restricted species and their blocks; the project statuses (Draft and Under review stay: changing what is published is the designer's call); the Western Grey Kangaroo's scientific name (the data names it `Macropus giganteus`, the Eastern Grey; the Western Grey is `Macropus fuliginosus`), which was already so.
  - **Not checked.** The reports (the Species detail, Specimen and Consolidated reports read these records; not opened with the new rows); the Home dashboard's record counts; the static build with the new record pages (about 32 more); the project pages of Mount Remarkable and Naracoorte beyond the counts.
  - **Open, for the designer.** Whether the Coorong, Flinders and Nullarbor projects should be published (so their records, the 10 km blocks among them, show in Explore). More species spreads (the plan named six; flora such as the Golden Wattle could spread too). The kangaroo's scientific name.
