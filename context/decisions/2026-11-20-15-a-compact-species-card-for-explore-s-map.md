# 2026-11-20 - A compact species card for Explore's Map and table view

- **Nov 20 2026: the summary card has a compact form for the split view.** The designer: "We'll need a compressed or smaller species card that works well with the table view." The job: someone with the table below and a short map above clicks a dot or hovers a row and needs to know what the record is, without the card cutting off (`2026-11-20-14`). Principles at stake: say less, and reveal in steps (the photo and the facts that do not fit are the first to go); they pull against make it obvious, since the photo is how a species is recognised, so it stays, small.
  - **What changed.** `RecordPeekCard` takes an opt-in `compact` (1.6; the full card is the default and is unchanged). Compact draws the photo as a 64px thumbnail beside the eyebrow, name and scientific name (the existing `SpeciesPhoto`, as the result list's rows do, 0.9), then the project line, then the facts side by side, each label over its value (`SummaryFacts inline`, one hairline above, a fourth fact wraps to a second row), then the restricted-location note. The card has no tall photo, and keeps the `ScrollFade` of `-14` for a window too short even for this. Explore passes `compact` while the table is open (`split`); the map view keeps the full card.
  - **Measured** (registered user, search "Malleefowl", the tightest case: a restricted record, so the note shows): the card is 287px, the column 300px: no scroll and no fade at 1716 by 1034; at 1440 by 800 the card is 181px and the details scroll with the fade, as designed. The full card in map view is unchanged (271px for a record with no photo). Zero console errors. `tsc`, `eslint` and `check:contracts` clean. Not checked: touch, the project card in the split view (the table view lists species only), a species with two or more photos (the carousel is not in the compact card, which shows the first).
  - **Open, for the designer (0.4).** The compact card's thumbnail has no photo credit, where the full card's info icon names the photographer and licence (the licences ask for a credit wherever a photo is shown; the result list's thumbnails have none either, so this follows them). Whether the thumbnail should carry the credit as a tooltip, and whether the second and later photos matter in this view, are the designer's calls.
